32.1.7 R32.2: 32.2.3 to 32.2.5 Fixed Issue Details MR-99882 - Clients may experience latency spikes and packet loss on 5 GHz Potential symptoms Clients connected on 5 GHz may experience intermittent latency spikes, packet loss, and substantially reduced throughput while remaining associated. The degradation is especially apparent in latency-sensitive real-time video applications such as Microsoft Teams, where audio or video may pause, stutter, or become unusable. Webpages and other network applications may also load slowly or fail to complete. The condition can affect a subset of APs, persist after client counts fall, and temporarily clear after an AP restart or channel change. Conditions The issue is intermittent and may require prolonged operation before it appears. Engineering reproduced it on an MR46 serving eight 5 GHz clients with continuous bidirectional conferencing-like audio and video traffic while an adjacent-channel AP served 15 additional loaded clients, for 23 clients across the two APs. Reports also include high-density deployments and APs serving only a few clients, including periods without high channel utilization or obvious RF interference. Disabling downlink beamforming and OFDMA did not prevent the reported behavior. Engineering analysis associates the degradation with radio transmission failures during single-user transmit beamforming, but the exact initiating sequence and time to onset remain variable. Resolution Updated radio software corrects three recovery paths used during single-user transmit beamforming. It delays recovery until in-flight beamforming packets are transmitted, restores affected transmit parameters when their state becomes invalid, and recovers radio state when beamforming weights are unavailable. These corrections prevent the transmit path from remaining in the degraded state associated with sustained latency and packet loss. Workarounds Restarting the AP or changing its channel may temporarily restore performance; either action disconnects clients, and degradation may recur. Downgrading to a compatible MR 30 or MR 31 release has restored service in reported deployments. Recent engineering comparisons use MR 32.1.7 as an earlier baseline, but recovery on that release is not established for every affected deployment. Contact Meraki Support to select a compatible previously working release, confirm recovery, and obtain current mitigation guidance. Firmware changes interrupt wireless service. No configuration-only workaround is confirmed. Confirmed affected models MR44, MR46, MR56, MR57, CW9164I, CW9166I Confirmed affected versions R32.2: 32.2.4 to 32.2.5 R33.1: 33.1.3 Back to release summary MR-104909 - Clients may experience severe 5 GHz latency and packet loss on a DFS channel Potential symptoms Clients on an affected 5 GHz radio may experience multi-second latency and repeated packet loss, including when reaching the AP itself or the default gateway. The behavior can affect all clients connected to the same AP and interrupt real-time applications or ordinary data transfers. Conditions The confirmed failure involves 5 GHz DFS operation with OFDMA enabled and neighboring beacons that do not advertise tolerance for narrow-band OFDMA uplink responses. The AP automatically restricts those narrow responses as part of DFS coexistence handling; an actual radar detection or a manual configuration change is not required. With a 20 MHz uplink response bandwidth and clients using dynamic spatial-multiplexing power save, the affected scheduling path can retain more clients in a group than the permitted response allocations can accommodate. Invalid, overlapping allocations cause failed exchanges and load-dependent latency or packet loss. Reports include Windows clients, including a Wi-Fi 6E client; broader client applicability is not fully established. The behavior can persist with single-user transmit beamforming disabled and return within a few minutes of a reboot, distinguishing it from the separate beamforming-related latency issue MR-99882. Resolution The correction makes OFDMA client grouping respect the uplink response resources permitted by the radio's current coexistence restrictions. It reduces the affected group when the available response bandwidth cannot accommodate all scheduled clients, preventing invalid, overlapping allocations and the failed exchanges that delay data traffic. Normal DFS coexistence protections remain in effect. Workarounds Use a non-DFS channel for the affected 5 GHz radio. In Dashboard, go to Wireless > Configure > Radio Settings and either manually select a permitted non-DFS channel or exclude DFS channels from the assigned RF profile. For the RF-profile option, use "Change channels used by AutoChannel…" and "Deselect DFS Channels", then save. Confirm the radio is operating on a permitted non-DFS channel. Channel changes can briefly interrupt client connectivity, so coordinate an appropriate maintenance window. Excluding DFS channels reduces the available channel pool and requires reviewing channel reuse and capacity; a manual override also gives up automatic channel selection for that radio. Rebooting the AP does not provide lasting recovery in the reported case, and disabling single-user transmit beamforming does not prevent the behavior. Contact Meraki Support for current mitigation guidance and assistance collecting evidence while the issue is present. See the Cisco Meraki DFS channel guidance for configuration steps. Back to release summary MR-89999 - Multicast speaker streams may fail on Wi-Fi Personal Network SSIDs Potential symptoms Speaker groups may stop delivering audio to member speakers when their stream changes from unicast to multicast. Conditions Wi-Fi Personal Network (WPN) is enabled on the SSID, and the speakers belong to the same WPN group. The failure is associated with multicast distribution between group members. The exact forwarding failure remains under investigation; toggling multicast-to-unicast conversion did not restore the reported stream. Confirmed affected models Exact AP model scope is not fully established Confirmed affected versions R31.1: 31.1.8 R32.1: 32.1.6, 32.1.7 R32.2: 32.2.3 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds Disabling the Dashboard control "Wi-Fi Personal Network (WPN)" on the affected SSID has restored streaming. This removes WPN's per-user segmentation, so assess the isolation requirements before making the change and coordinate it with Meraki Support. Changes to the SSID can disrupt connected clients. See Wi-Fi Personal Network documentation for the isolation behavior to assess before disabling WPN. Engineering is validating the delivered power-limit tables and conducted versus radiated measurements. Any coordinated channel or width change can interrupt clients and alter coverage or capacity. Back to release summary MR-96859 - CW9166I APs may overreport non-802.11 utilization on 6 GHz Potential symptoms Dashboard may show high 6 GHz non-802.11 utilization on CW9166I APs even when an external spectrum check finds no corresponding non-Wi-Fi interferer. Reports are most apparent during busy periods in dense deployments, and the measurement can give administrators a misleading indication of external interference. Conditions Reports involve dense 6 GHz deployments in the UK/ETSI regulatory domain. Sensitive radio energy-detection settings can count distant Wi-Fi transmissions as non-802.11 energy when their preambles cannot be decoded. The reporting behavior and suitable permanent threshold settings remain under validation. Confirmed affected models CW9166I Confirmed affected versions R31.1: 31.1.8 R32.2: 32.2.4 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds Contact Meraki Support to assess a temporary radio energy-detection adjustment. Limited field testing on MR 32.2.4 reduced the reported non-802.11 utilization, but the adjustment is lost after an AP reboot and firmware integration still requires validation. Corroborate the reported utilization with spectrum measurements before treating it as evidence of a non-Wi-Fi interferer. Back to release summary MR-103372 - Local Auth clients using OKC may disconnect after approximately three hours Potential symptoms Clients using Local Auth with Opportunistic Key Caching (OKC) may be deauthenticated after approximately 2 hours and 59 minutes and then need to authenticate again. This interrupts client connectivity more frequently than the 12-hour session behavior reported with Fast Transition. Conditions The issue is reported on a Local Auth SSID where the authentication response sets a Session-Timeout of 10,740 seconds. The AP expires the client's cached authentication state at that interval. The exact AP model scope is not yet established. Confirmed affected models Exact AP model scope is not fully established Confirmed affected versions R32.2: 32.2.4 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds Enabling the Dashboard "802.11r" control (Fast Transition) on the affected SSID has increased the reported session period to 12 hours. Validate client compatibility before enabling it and coordinate the SSID change with Meraki Support; configuration changes can interrupt service, and session expiry is delayed rather than eliminated. Back to release summary MR-93209 - Wi-Fi 7 APs may send QoS data frames towards the end client STA with To DS bit 1, and From DS bit 1 Potential symptoms A wireless client may stop receiving usable downstream traffic while remaining associated. macOS clients may require a manual Wi-Fi reconnect, while some Windows clients detect the lost gateway reachability and reconnect automatically. Conditions The issue is confirmed on CW9172I and CW9176I during sustained same-AP traffic at 80 MHz channel width, often reproducing one to two minutes into a high-throughput test. A received four-address wireless frame can cause the AP radio to apply an inappropriate forwarding mode and subsequently send QoS data frames with both distribution-system direction bits set; meshing must be enabled for the affected path. Confirmed affected models CW9172I, CW9176I Confirmed affected versions R31.1: 31.1.8 R32.1: 32.1.6 R32.2: 32.2.3 to 32.2.5, 32.2.5.1 Potential workarounds Disable meshing to avoid the affected forwarding path, or disconnect and reconnect an impacted client to restore traffic temporarily. Disabling meshing removes wireless repeater capability and should be evaluated against deployment requirements. Back to release summary MR-105061 - CW9176I clients may experience high 5 GHz latency with downlink OFDMA Potential symptoms Clients may experience elevated 5 GHz latency, slow application performance, degraded or dropped voice and video calls, and interrupted file transfers. Recorded latency reaches hundreds of milliseconds to several seconds. Severity increases with client activity and can subside as activity decreases without rebooting the AP. Conditions The behavior is confirmed on a 5 GHz WLAN with a high concentration of 802.11ax clients and traffic that favors downlink Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access (OFDMA), including small-payload traffic. Reports involve Windows clients using Intel Wi-Fi 6E AX211 adapters; applicability to other client types is not yet established. Client count alone does not determine whether the issue occurs: latency is observed with fewer than 20 associated clients as well as at higher counts. The underlying scheduling behavior remains under investigation. Confirmed affected models CW9176I Confirmed affected versions R31.1: 31.1.8 R32.1: 32.1.7 R32.2: 32.2.5, 32.2.5.1 Potential workarounds Contact Meraki Support to disable downlink OFDMA on the affected SSIDs using a Support-managed configuration. This mitigates the reported latency even with more than 30 active clients. Applying the change restarts the affected radios and briefly disconnects clients, so coordinate a suitable maintenance window. The mitigation disables simultaneous OFDMA downlink transmissions for those SSIDs and can reduce airtime efficiency; validate application performance after the change. Back to release summary MR-93211 - MR44, CW9162I, and CW9172I APs may experience an unexpected reboot Potential symptoms MR44, CW9162I, and CW9172I APs may experience an unexpected reboot, disconnecting associated clients and temporarily making wireless service unavailable while the AP restarts. Conditions The issue occurs intermittently during client reassociation activity involving Protected Management Frames (PMF). Available engineering evidence associates it with incomplete cleanup of a previous protected client association during rapid reassociation, but the exact traffic sequence needed to trigger it is not fully characterized. Confirmed affected models MR44, CW9162I, CW9172I Confirmed affected versions R32.1: 32.1.5 to 32.1.7 R32.2: 32.2.1 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds No confirmed configuration workaround is available. The AP recovers by rebooting, but associated clients are disconnected during the restart. Contact Meraki Support if repeated reboots occur. Back to release summary MR-101697 - CW9172I APs may experience an unexpected reboot Potential symptoms CW9172I APs may experience an unexpected reboot, temporarily interrupting wireless connectivity for associated clients. Conditions A radio-firmware failure is associated with these reboots. Similar diagnostic signatures can represent different failure paths, and no specific customer configuration or traffic sequence is confirmed as a trigger. Confirmed affected models CW9172I Confirmed affected versions R32.2: 32.2.4 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds No confirmed configuration workaround is available. Contact Meraki Support if reboots recur so the diagnostic signature can be confirmed and appropriate firmware guidance provided. Back to release summary MR-86484 - MR44, CW9172I, and CW9176I APs may experience an unexpected reboot Potential symptoms MR44, CW9172I, and CW9176I APs may experience an unexpected reboot, disconnecting associated clients and temporarily interrupting wireless service while the AP restarts. Conditions Available engineering evidence includes more than one timer-related failure path. Some reports involve cleanup of Protected Management Frames (PMF) security-query timers, while other reports have a different timer-failure signature. A specific configuration or traffic trigger is not confirmed, and matching reboot reports require Support correlation. Confirmed affected models MR44, CW9172I, CW9176I Confirmed affected versions R32.1: 32.1.6, 32.1.7 R32.2: 32.2.3 to 32.2.5 Potential workarounds No confirmed configuration workaround is available. Contact Meraki Support if repeated reboots occur so the failure path can be identified and an appropriate software recommendation provided. Each reboot interrupts service to associated clients.

   <H2 id="toc-hId--679218596">Fixed Issue Details</H2>
   <DIV class="">
    <H3 id="toc-hId-11342878"><A href="https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Product_Information/Compatibility_and_Firmware/MR_32.2.5.1_Release_Notes?_gl=1*yqvkjj*_ga*MTAxMTg5MzEzNS4xNzY4MDczODM4*_ga_DG2DVWGCGW*czE3OTM5MTcyNzMkbzIzOSRnMSR0MTc5MzkxNzMwOSRqMjQkbDAkaDA.#mr-99882" target="_blank" rel="internal noopener nofollow noreferrer">MR-99882</A> - Clients may experience latency spikes and packet loss on 5 GHz</H3>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId-701904352">Potential symptoms</H4>
     <P>Clients connected on 5 GHz may experience intermittent latency spikes, packet loss, and substantially reduced throughput while remaining associated. The degradation is especially apparent in latency-sensitive real-time video applications such as Microsoft Teams, where audio or video may pause, stutter, or become unusable. Webpages and other network applications may also load slowly or fail to complete. The condition can affect a subset of APs, persist after client counts fall, and temporarily clear after an AP restart or channel change.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId--1105550111">Conditions</H4>
     <P>The issue is intermittent and may require prolonged operation before it appears. Engineering reproduced it on an MR46 serving eight 5 GHz clients with continuous bidirectional conferencing-like audio and video traffic while an adjacent-channel AP served 15 additional loaded clients, for 23 clients across the two APs. Reports also include high-density deployments and APs serving only a few clients, including periods without high channel utilization or obvious RF interference. Disabling downlink beamforming and OFDMA did not prevent the reported behavior. Engineering analysis associates the degradation with radio transmission failures during single-user transmit beamforming, but the exact initiating sequence and time to onset remain variable.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId-1381962722">Resolution</H4>
     <P>Updated radio software corrects three recovery paths used during single-user transmit beamforming. It delays recovery until in-flight beamforming packets are transmitted, restores affected transmit parameters when their state becomes invalid, and recovers radio state when beamforming weights are unavailable. These corrections prevent the transmit path from remaining in the degraded state associated with sustained latency and packet loss.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId--425491741">Workarounds</H4>
     <P>Restarting the AP or changing its channel may temporarily restore performance; either action disconnects clients, and degradation may recur. Downgrading to a compatible MR 30 or MR 31 release has restored service in reported deployments. Recent engineering comparisons use MR 32.1.7 as an earlier baseline, but recovery on that release is not established for every affected deployment. Contact Meraki Support to select a compatible previously working release, confirm recovery, and obtain current mitigation guidance. Firmware changes interrupt wireless service. No configuration-only workaround is confirmed.</P>
    </DIV>

    <H3 id="toc-hId--451099954"><A href="https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Product_Information/Compatibility_and_Firmware/MR_32.2.5.1_Release_Notes?_gl=1*yqvkjj*_ga*MTAxMTg5MzEzNS4xNzY4MDczODM4*_ga_DG2DVWGCGW*czE3OTM5MTcyNzMkbzIzOSRnMSR0MTc5MzkxNzMwOSRqMjQkbDAkaDA.#mr-104909" target="_blank" rel="internal noopener nofollow noreferrer">MR-104909</A> - Clients may experience severe 5 GHz latency and packet loss on a DFS channel</H3>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId--2129471698">Potential symptoms</H4>
     <P>Clients on an affected 5 GHz radio may experience multi-second latency and repeated packet loss, including when reaching the AP itself or the default gateway. The behavior can affect all clients connected to the same AP and interrupt real-time applications or ordinary data transfers.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId-358041135">Conditions</H4>
     <P>The confirmed failure involves 5 GHz DFS operation with OFDMA enabled and neighboring beacons that do not advertise tolerance for narrow-band OFDMA uplink responses. The AP automatically restricts those narrow responses as part of DFS coexistence handling; an actual radar detection or a manual configuration change is not required. With a 20 MHz uplink response bandwidth and clients using dynamic spatial-multiplexing power save, the affected scheduling path can retain more clients in a group than the permitted response allocations can accommodate. Invalid, overlapping allocations cause failed exchanges and load-dependent latency or packet loss. Reports include Windows clients, including a Wi-Fi 6E client; broader client applicability is not fully established. The behavior can persist with single-user transmit beamforming disabled and return within a few minutes of a reboot, distinguishing it from the separate beamforming-related latency issue <A href="https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Product_Information/Compatibility_and_Firmware/MR_32.2.5.1_Release_Notes?_gl=1*yqvkjj*_ga*MTAxMTg5MzEzNS4xNzY4MDczODM4*_ga_DG2DVWGCGW*czE3OTM5MTcyNzMkbzIzOSRnMSR0MTc5MzkxNzMwOSRqMjQkbDAkaDA.#mr-99882" target="_blank" rel="internal noopener nofollow noreferrer">MR-99882</A>.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId--1449413328">Resolution</H4>
     <P>The correction makes OFDMA client grouping respect the uplink response resources permitted by the radio's current coexistence restrictions. It reduces the affected group when the available response bandwidth cannot accommodate all scheduled clients, preventing invalid, overlapping allocations and the failed exchanges that delay data traffic. Normal DFS coexistence protections remain in effect.</P>
    </DIV>
    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId-1038099505">Workarounds</H4>
     <P>Use a non-DFS channel for the affected 5 GHz radio. In Dashboard, go to Wireless &gt; Configure &gt; Radio Settings and either manually select a permitted non-DFS channel or exclude DFS channels from the assigned RF profile. For the RF-profile option, use "Change channels used by AutoChannel…" and "Deselect DFS Channels", then save. Confirm the radio is operating on a permitted non-DFS channel.</P>
     <P>Channel changes can briefly interrupt client connectivity, so coordinate an appropriate maintenance window. Excluding DFS channels reduces the available channel pool and requires reviewing channel reuse and capacity; a manual override also gives up automatic channel selection for that radio. Rebooting the AP does not provide lasting recovery in the reported case, and disabling single-user transmit beamforming does not prevent the behavior. Contact Meraki Support for current mitigation guidance and assistance collecting evidence while the issue is present. See the <A href="https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Operate_and_Maintain/User_Guides/Radio_Settings/Dynamic_Frequency_Selection_(DFS)" target="_blank" rel="internal noopener nofollow noreferrer">Cisco Meraki DFS channel guidance</A> for configuration steps.</P>
    </DIV>

    <DIV class="">
     <H4 id="toc-hId--1214066905">Conditions</H4>
     <P>The behavior is confirmed on a 5 GHz WLAN with a high concentration of 802.11ax clients and traffic that favors downlink Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access (OFDMA), including small-payload traffic. Reports involve Windows clients using Intel Wi-Fi 6E AX211 adapters; applicability to other client types is not yet established. Client count alone does not determine whether the issue occurs: latency is observed with fewer than 20 associated clients as well as at higher counts. The underlying scheduling behavior remains under investigation.</P>
